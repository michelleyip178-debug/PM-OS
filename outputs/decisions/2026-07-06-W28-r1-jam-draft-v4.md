## What's changed since the jam — the real update

**1. The ATS fork resolved itself, and made Epic C harder, not easier.**
The jam treated World A vs. World B as an open decision with a recommendation. It's no longer a decision — Engineering confirmed ATS integration won't be ready until 2028 (e-tender process), sourced fact as of 6 Jul. This actually simplifies the SteerCo sign-off conversation (native tracking matches Mark's original 5 Jun ask), but it means Epic C's manager-facing status-update UX is now **entirely new, unscoped build** — the jam's "the seam is the biggest undiscovered surface" call turned out to be exactly right, and it's now flagged Red risk in the parent PRD.

**2. The jam's three open dependencies are now three explicit blockers with no dates.**
The jam named these as open questions to flag. Since then, personas, job stories, and effort sizing have all independently converged on the same three items as the load-bearing risk in R1:
- Agency-admin auth (Epic A) — unchanged since the jam, still no owner commitment.
- Competency SSOT contract #18/#41 (Epic B pre-fill) — sourcing resolved (Imelda's workstream, 5 Jul), but the endpoint payload spec between Léo and Kingsley is still open.
- Manager status-update UX (Epic C) — this is new since the jam; it didn't exist as a named risk until the ATS fork resolved to World B.

**3. Effort sizing shows the jam's epic prioritization was directionally right, but the "Must" bucket is only 40-60% sizeable today.**
The jam's MoSCoW (Must: A-narrow + B + C) still holds. What's new: the effort-sizing analysis quantifies that each Must epic's hardest, most valuable piece sits in an unsizeable "unknown" bucket — auth (A), pre-fill (B), manager UX (C). Total estimable now: ~36-64 points (~1.5-2.5 sprints, two devs). The actual number is plausibly 1.5-2x that. The jam couldn't have known this in June; it's only visible now that story-level job stories exist.

**4. Persona and journey work sharpened *why* Epic D is fragile, beyond "it's a Should."**
The jam already flagged D's "resume application" half as data-gated (>40% abandonment). What's new: the journey map shows Epic D is Lane 2's only mechanism for feeding Lane 1 — R1's entire funnel-widening play. If D gets cut (still the first-cut candidate under scope pressure), that's not just losing a convenience feature, it's losing the on-ramp to R1's primary success metric.

**5. One jam scope boundary needs re-confirming: SJR.**
The jam said "SJR: NOT in R1 creation unless Mark explicitly pulls it in." The parent PRD's open questions (6 Jul) still list "SJR Apply Flow Scope: are SJRs fully in R1 scope now?" as unresolved — so this hasn't drifted, but it also hasn't closed. Worth a direct answer before SteerCo, since it's been open since before the jam.
