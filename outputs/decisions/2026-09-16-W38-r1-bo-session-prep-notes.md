## R1 BO Session — Prep Notes (Internal Only)

Companion to the [sharable brief](2026-09-16-W38-r1-bo-alignment-brief.md). Your script for the room.

**Watch for:** every recommendation is also the cheapest option for us to build. A sharp BO will notice and ask you to justify it on their goals, not our timeline. Lead with their incentive; keep cost as backup.

**If time runs short, prioritize #5 and #6** — highest risk of a "yes" in the room quietly failing in practice. #1/2/7/8 are contained. #3/4 can run on a longer clock, but #4 needs a scheduled follow-up. #9/10 aren't blocking today.

### #5 — Standard form policy (highest stakes)

**Pushback:** "You're asking us to pre-commit to backing you against agencies we haven't heard from yet."

They're right to resist a blank check — don't ask for one. Ask instead for a **standard response script**: *"CareerCompass supports one optional question by design; anything beyond that routes to the agency's existing form."* That's repeatable policy, not a case-by-case fight each time.

**Get this in writing.** A verbal nod here is exactly what erodes the first time an agency pushes back hard. If you leave without a written line or agreed script, treat it as unresolved.

### #6 — Hard cutover vs. dual-posting bridge

**Pushback:** BOs will lean toward the bridge — it's the lower-risk-feeling option for them, since no agency visibly loses access.

Your counter needs to be sharper than "dilutes traffic": **the bridge isn't a one-time cost — it's ongoing two-way sync work, indefinitely, that was never part of this release's build.** If they pick the bridge, that's new scope, not a softer version of the cutover. Say so explicitly before it gets decided as the "safe" default.

---

### #9 / #10 — Not yet theirs to own

Neither will get pushback because they're new to the room — that's the risk, not a good sign. Make clear both are **BO decisions**, not engineering defaults:
- **#9:** if unresolved, officer access can change with zero visible record for PSD.
- **#10:** "clean up personal data" has real privacy implications — route to Legal, don't let it default to whoever builds the feature.
