What HR isn't doing here

Everything. No approval chains, no role-based posting permissions, no supervisor pre-clearance, no departmental approval queues, and (corrected 23 Sep) no RBAC design, no audit log review, no orphan-posting fallback either. HR has zero role in STIPs & Gigs, not a smaller one. This is the real contrast with IJR and SJR, which make HR the engine (screening, pooling, matching, approving, re-integrating): STIPs & Gigs makes HR nothing.

The gap this leaves open

RBAC/access-control design, audit log review, and the orphan-posting fallback still need to happen, they just don't have an owner. That's R-27: a genuinely open question, not a "system handles it automatically" resolution. If nobody's named, postings from departed officers could sit live indefinitely with no one accountable for closing them.

The real contrast with IJR and SJR: those programmes make HR the engine, screening, pooling, matching, approving, re-integrating. STIPs and Gigs makes HR nothing: no engine, no guardrail, no role of any kind. RBAC, an audit log, and a fallback for orphaned postings still need an owner, they just don't have one right now.

Where HR won't see a problem until it's already happened: this used to describe HR's blind spots. It's now moot: there's no HR function here to have a blind spot in. The blind spot belongs to whoever eventually gets named to RBAC, the audit log, and orphan postings, and until then it belongs to no one.

Ownership gap flagged 23 Sep, R-27: RBAC/access-control design, audit log review, and the orphan-posting fallback (when a poster leaves or changes agency before closing their posting) are unassigned. This is a real operational gap, not a documentation cleanup, and it isn't resolved just by removing HR from the picture.

Governance load rises with the population decision that's still pending: a WOG-wide population means more postings, more applications, and more edge cases (orphaned postings, access disputes) than a 6-agency pilot would, and right now there's no owner to catch any of it.

Four things still not locked in: Reporting Officer awareness, auto-expiry, the target apply time, poster alerts. All of these sit with the poster or applicant, HR was never part of that loop.
