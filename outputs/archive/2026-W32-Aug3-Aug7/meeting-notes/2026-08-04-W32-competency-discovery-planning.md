## Risks Not Adequately Addressed

*(Preserved from source material — risks that emerged from the transcript but weren't directly discussed in the meeting.)*

1. **Discovery may become stakeholder-led rather than user-led.** Interview targets are almost entirely policy owners, governance teams, and HR stakeholders — with little discussion of actual end users or officers. Risk: findings describe "how the process was designed" rather than "how the process actually works." The Kevin/EDB lead is the strongest mitigation attempt so far, but it's one lead, not a plan.

2. **Interview sample bias.** Proposed participants cluster around WD, CDGO, and PSD HR — with no discussion of ministries with low competency maturity, ministries that struggle with tagging, or agencies with poor adoption. Current sample risks producing an overly optimistic picture of how competency tagging actually works in practice.

3. **No explicit stopping criteria.** The team discussed session count (one, two, maybe three) but not how they'll know discovery is complete or what evidence would be sufficient. Without this, discovery can expand indefinitely.

4. **Governance perspective may dominate.** Most proposed interviews focus on governance, approvals, and policy ownership — with less emphasis on tagging effort, operational burden, or day-to-day user workflow. A common discovery failure mode: producing a governance model instead of solving the actual operational pain point.

5. **Dependence on stakeholder introductions.** Several critical stakeholders are only reachable through Xian Zhang Guo, PSD HR contacts, or Kevin. Any delay in those introductions blocks progress — and per the action items table above, "Identify PSD HR contacts" is still listed as Open.

## PM Readout (from source material)

If stepping in as PM, the biggest gap isn't stakeholder access — the team has a reasonable path to interviews already. The bigger unanswered questions, to resolve **before** interviews start:

1. **What decision will discovery enable?** New capability, process redesign, or governance change?
2. **What assumptions are we trying to invalidate?** E.g., competency tagging is inaccurate; HR finds tagging burdensome; governance slows adoption.
3. **What evidence would be enough to stop discovery?** E.g., 5 ministries, 3 HR teams, 2 governance owners.
4. **Are we optimising for policy intent or operational reality?** Current plan leans heavily toward policy/governance stakeholders.

Answering these four before interviews begin significantly reduces the risk of gathering a lot of information without knowing what product or process decision it should drive.
