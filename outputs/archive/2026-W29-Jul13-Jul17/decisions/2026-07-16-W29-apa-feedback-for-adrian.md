## Areas for Consideration

**1. The indirect reporting line creates ambiguity on when something should go to you directly.**
Because day-to-day contact runs through Jace, it's not always clear when a time-sensitive item should be escalated straight to Adrian versus routed and waited on. The clearest example: the October-to-November go-live slip surfaced at squad sync in late June, and there wasn't a clear answer on whose job it was to bring that to Adrian directly given the external date impact. A standing rule (e.g., anything with external-date impact reaches Adrian within 48 hours, PM's discretion on channel) would remove the need to re-litigate this every time.

**2. Some cross-team blockers on CareerCompass surface without a clear owner, and default to sitting open.**
Agency-admin auth is a good example: it's genuinely an engineering/access question (Pow Hwee/Fabian's call to make), but it's been flagged repeatedly as the hardest gate blocking Epic A and has stayed unowned for weeks rather than landing with anyone. As product lead for CareerCompass, I think this is squarely in Adrian's remit to force, not just PM-level chasing: a push to get someone to explicitly accept ownership (even if the answer is "not me, here's who") would stop it from drifting until it's the thing blocking a squad's roadmap.

**3. Recognition lands well when it happens, but could be more consistent.**
The R1 competitive analysis got a genuine, public callout at PM Weekly, and that kind of visible recognition is valuable both for morale and as a signal to the wider team about what good work looks like. It doesn't happen as consistently across other work streams, and a bit more of it, especially for less visible groundwork, would go a long way.
