Adrian's comment about "articulating product discovery" is close to a direct signal about what the APA reviewer will be looking for.

## Decisions Made

*(No formal decisions — this was guidance/expectation-setting, not a decision-making session.)*

1. **APA assessments must explicitly show product discovery process, not just outcomes.**
   - **Why:** Adrian's stated expectation — PMs need to articulate *how* they arrived at a sharpened problem statement, not just present the resulting decision or feature.
   - **Who decided:** Adrian (guidance, not a joint decision).
   - **Impact:** Directly affects how the Impact and Craft & Execution sections of the APA writeup should be structured — the discovery process itself needs to be visible as evidence, not just referenced in passing.

2. **Backlog prioritization must be defensible with data, not BO preference.**
   - **Why:** Adrian's concern — if priority calls are made based on "what BOs want," the team loses the ability to defend those calls under scrutiny, and it signals reactive prioritization rather than owned strategic judgment.
   - **Who decided:** Adrian (guidance).
   - **Impact:** This echoes Barry's scope-creep concern pattern (raised earlier this week on R1 and CMM) — both are versions of "is this decision being made on evidence, or because someone with influence asked for it." Worth treating as a recurring theme rather than two separate concerns.
