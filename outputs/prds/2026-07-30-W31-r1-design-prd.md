## The Problem, in One Paragraph

Today, an officer can find an opportunity on CareerCompass, but the moment they click Apply, they leave the platform — redirected to an external form, retyping everything from scratch, and then hearing nothing back. From CareerCompass's point of view, that officer just vanished. R1 closes that loop: officers apply without leaving, the form already knows who they are, and they can see what happens to their application afterward. For HR, the same idea applies to posting a job in the first place — right now that happens somewhere else and gets imported in; R1 lets HR create and manage the posting natively too.

---

## Three Journeys This Release Touches

### Journey 1 — Officer applies to something

**Today:** Officer finds a posting → clicks Apply → gets redirected to FormSG or Careers@Gov → fills out a blank form manually → submits → hears nothing.

**R1:** Officer finds a posting → clicks Apply → a form opens *inside* CareerCompass, already filled in with what the platform knows about them (competencies, work history) → they review and adjust anything wrong → submit → they can check back and see where their application stands.

**What needs design:**
- The apply form itself — pre-filled fields, editable, clear about what's pre-filled vs. what needs input
- A "My Applications" view showing status (Submitted → Under Review → Outcome)
- **The rejection/outcome screen** — flagged explicitly as the most emotionally sensitive moment in the whole release. This needs its own careful design pass, not a generic "sorry, not this time" banner. Owner: Amber, alongside the underlying status-state data model, not after it's built.

### Journey 2 — HR posts an opportunity

**Today:** HR posts in an external system (OTG), then separately fields applications by email, then tracks progress in a spreadsheet — three disconnected tools for one job.

**R1:** HR creates the posting directly in CareerCompass, publishes it, and later manages applicants through the same status states officers see on their end (Submitted → Under Review → Outcome).

**What needs design:**
- A creation form covering six posting types: Internal Job, Secondment, STIP, Gig, Internal Rotation, and SJR
- **This is genuinely new, undesigned territory** — nothing like a manager-facing "move this applicant along" interface has existed before, because previously an external ATS was going to own this. There's no existing pattern to extend.
- One open design question that affects the form itself: how do we help HR pick the right posting type when some of these types can look similar on the surface (e.g., "Internal Rotation" vs. "Secondment")? A proposed distinction is grouping by *long-term stint* (Job, Secondment, Internal Rotation, SJR) vs. *short-term/gig* (STIP, Gig) — worth considering as a first filter in the creation flow, though it won't fully disambiguate within the long-term group on its own.

### Journey 3 — The quiet middle (the seam)

Between "officer submits" and "officer sees a status update," there's a manager on the other side reviewing and moving things along. **Both sides of this go quiet at exactly the same point today, and there's no existing UX for the manager's side at all.** This is flagged as the single highest-risk undesigned surface in the release — not because it's hard, but because it's easy to underestimate. It needs its own design pass before anything here gets sized or built, not discovered midway through.

---

## One Thing That's Being Built But Won't Launch Yet

SJR (one of the six posting types above) is being built now with the same care as the rest, but it stays switched off until a 2028 cycle — it's foundation work, not a Jan 2027 launch feature. For design, this mostly means: design it properly like the other five types, but don't assume real usage or feedback on it will come back before 2028. Treat it as "designed and shelved," not "designed and validated."

---

## Design Priorities, In Order

1. **The rejection/outcome screen** (Journey 1) — most emotionally loaded moment, needs to be designed alongside the underlying data model, not bolted on after.
2. **The manager status-update interface** (Journey 2/3, "the seam") — completely new territory, no existing pattern to build from. This blocks sizing the whole status-tracking piece of work.
3. **The six-type creation form** (Journey 2) — needs to handle real ambiguity between similar-sounding posting types without making HR guess.

---

## What This Release Is Not Trying To Fix

Worth knowing so scope doesn't quietly creep: this release doesn't touch the bigger discovery problem — officers still may look across multiple platforms to find opportunities in the first place, categorisation inconsistency across agencies isn't being solved here, and smarter competency-based matching/recommendations are a later release. This release is specifically about what happens *after* someone already found something: applying, and knowing what happened next.
