To Business Owners — OTEP / OTG Platform From Product team Date 9 June 2026 Reading time ~10 minutes

OTEP is 4 decisions away from being ready to launch

We've completed a full analysis of the current opportunity data. The platform is ready — but 75% of open opportunities will not appear on it unless we resolve a small number of policy questions that only you can answer. This document explains the situation and asks for your decisions.

Bottom line up front
Of 633 open opportunities in the system, only 160 will appear on OTEP at launch under current rules. The remaining 473 are blocked because required information is missing or their opportunity type is unclear. Agencies need to fix their data — but before we can ask them to, we need you to confirm four business rules. This document walks through each one.

What is happening

OTEP imports opportunities from the OTG system. For an opportunity to appear on the platform, it needs a complete set of information — things like a start date, a function, and a clearly labelled opportunity type. If any of these are missing, the opportunity is blocked from appearing.

We've run that check across all 633 open opportunities in the system. The results show a significant data quality gap — not because agencies have been careless, but because the OTG system has never enforced these requirements at the point of creation. Agencies posted opportunities the way the system allowed them to, and now we're asking OTEP to work with that data.

The four decisions we need from you

1
Is time commitment required for opportunities tagged [agilePSD] and similar programme names?
Affects 109 opportunities — whether they are blocked or cleared
Background. We've confirmed the rule for most types: time commitment is required for Gigs and STIPs (so applicants know how much of their time is involved), and not required for Secondments, Jobs, and SJRs (where the commitment is implicit in the role). The question is what to do with a group of 109 opportunities that use non-standard labels — things like [agilePSD-LDS], [KidSTART], [Overseas Posting], and [MDDI Internal Opportunity]. These appear to be job or secondment sub-programmes, but we haven't been able to confirm this.
Your options
A
Treat them like Jobs and Secondments — time commitment not required Recommended Unlocks up to 80 more opportunities
The naming and context of these programmes (internal transfers, overseas attachments, structured rotations) strongly suggest they are job or secondment equivalents. If you agree, we treat time commitment as not required for these types and they will clear ingestion on their other fields alone.
B
Treat them like Gigs — time commitment required Keeps 109 opportunities blocked
If these opportunities genuinely involve a short-term, flexible commitment similar to a Gig, then applicants need to know the time involved. Agencies for these 109 opportunities would need to fill in the time commitment field before their opportunities can appear.
C
Decide programme by programme Partial unlock
If the programmes are genuinely different in nature, we can handle them individually. This requires a short review of each of the 6 programme types involved. We can walk you through them if helpful.
What we need from you A ruling on whether the agilePSD, KidSTART, MDDI Internal, ITM, Overseas Posting, OCMO 2026, and PSFG programmes require time commitment — or whether they should be treated as Job/Secondment equivalents.
2
Should opportunities with a past closing date appear on OTEP?
Affects 346 open opportunities — whether they are live, hidden, or removed
Background. 346 of the 633 open opportunities have a closing date that has already passed. Some of these may be intentional — standing vacancies or evergreen roles where the "closing date" was a placeholder. Others are likely abandoned — 255 of them have never received a single application. If we ingest them as-is, applicants on OTEP may apply to opportunities that no longer exist. If we exclude them all, we lose over half our catalogue.
Your options
A
Exclude opportunities that are past their closing date AND have zero applicants Recommended Removes 255 opportunities
A opportunity that closed months ago and has never received a single application is very likely abandoned. Excluding this group protects applicant experience while keeping the 91 stale opportunities that have received at least one application — where an agency may have intentionally kept them open.
B
Exclude all opportunities with a past closing date Removes 346 opportunities from catalogue
Cleanest approach for applicant trust. No expired listing will ever appear. Downside: some genuinely open standing roles with outdated end dates will be removed. Agencies would need to update their closing dates to re-appear.
C
Ingest all open opportunities regardless of closing date, with a visual flag on OTEP Keeps all opportunities in catalogue
Show a banner on the listing card ("This opportunity may have closed — check with the agency before applying"). Preserves catalogue breadth but introduces applicant uncertainty. Requires a small change to the OTEP listing page design.
What we need from you A policy on stale opportunities: exclude all past their closing date, exclude only those with zero applicants, or ingest all with a visual flag.
3
What should happen to opportunities that have no opportunity type label?
Affects 78 opportunities — mostly from MSF (37) and Enterprise Singapore (19)
Background. OTEP uses the opportunity type (Gig, Secondment, Job, etc.) to help applicants filter and browse. 78 open opportunities don't have a type label at all — their names read like job titles with no prefix. Looking at the actual names, most of them from MSF and Enterprise Singapore appear to be Jobs or Secondments — they just weren't labelled that way when created. We can't automatically assign a type without confirmation.
Your options
A
Block them and ask agencies to add the correct label Recommended
The remediation report will flag these to the relevant agencies. MSF's 37 opportunities and Enterprise Singapore's 19 are clearly job-type roles — we can prompt them to add [Job] or [Secondment] to the title. This is the most accurate approach and takes no more than a few minutes per opportunity for an admin.
B
Ingest them as an "Unclassified" type visible on OTEP Unlocks 78 opportunities immediately
Applicants would see these opportunities but cannot filter by type. Pros: more catalogue content at launch. Cons: "Unclassified" is a poor applicant experience and makes filtering less useful. We'd recommend against this for launch.
C
Allow the product team to auto-assign [Job] to opportunities that look like job listings Unlocks ~60 opportunities with some risk
Based on the GigName patterns, we are fairly confident most unlabelled opportunities from MSF and Enterprise Singapore are Jobs. We could auto-classify them and flag to agencies for confirmation. Carries a small risk of mis-classification for a handful of opportunities.
What we need from you A decision on unlabelled opportunities: block until agencies fix them, ingest as Unclassified, or allow auto-assignment with agency confirmation.
4
Are start date and function required for every opportunity type — no exceptions?
Start date missing from 288 opportunities — the single largest blocker on the platform
Background. Start date is the #1 blocker — 288 opportunities are missing it. Function is missing from 174. Both are required in the current ingestion rules. However, some opportunity types may not have a meaningful start date — for example, a standing vacancy (like "we always need social workers") or an evergreen opening that rolls continuously. Similarly, some opportunities may span multiple functions. The question is whether to maintain the hard requirement or create an exception for certain opportunity types.
Your options
A
Keep both as hard requirements — no exceptions Recommended
Start date and function are the two most important filters for applicants. Without them, applicants cannot plan around an opportunity or find ones relevant to their work. The recommendation is to keep them mandatory and push agencies to fill them in. Agencies can use "flexible" or "to be confirmed" as a function if truly cross-functional.
B
Make start date optional for Jobs and Secondments only
Jobs and Secondments often have rolling start dates depending on when the candidate is available. If you agree these are genuinely open-ended, we could relax the start date requirement for these two types. This would unlock a significant number of opportunities but means applicants see roles with no timing information.
C
Remove function as a hard requirement (show it when available)
Function is used for filtering on the listing page. If it's missing, the opportunity simply won't appear in function-filtered searches — but it will still appear in unfiltered browsing. This is a softer approach: display function when available, but don't block the opportunity for lacking it.
What we need from you Confirm whether start date and function are hard requirements for all types — or whether either should be relaxed for specific opportunity types.
