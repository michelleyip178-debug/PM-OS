# GovTech PM Conversion Rubric & Process

**Source:** "Product Management Rubric and Final Conversion Process" (GovTech internal, shared 2026-07-30)

**Why this is saved:** This is the formal assessment rubric behind PM Level 2 conversion — answers the open question from the 2026-07-30 check-in with Jace about whether a formal rubric exists (it does, this is it).

---

## Who This Is For

Officers from: direct-mapping exercise, legacy PM Apprenticeship Programme, PM Conversion Pathway, or secondment postings re-entering GovTech. Requires explicit endorsement from Programme Director/CIO/CD, RO, and HRBP before starting — not opt-in.

---

## Assessment Philosophy

Same interview bar as external candidates — internal and external held to one standard. The central question every assessor answers: **what level of problem can this person solve?** Not "have they done the job," but do they frame problems at the right scope, connect execution to outcomes, and exercise judgment under ambiguity.

---

## The Mental Model — 4 Schema Dimensions

| Dimension | What It Means | What Assessors Look For |
|---|---|---|
| **Craft & Execution** | Technical/professional competence at level | Quality of thinking, rigour, depth of product/operational knowledge |
| **Ownership** | Accountability for outcomes not just tasks; learning mindset; judgment under pressure | Do they describe owning results or just inputs? Do they learn from failure? |
| **Strategic Alignment** | Connecting day-to-day work to team/agency/org goals | Do they articulate *why* something matters, not just what they did |
| **Culture & Organisational Influence** | How they work with others, communicate, shape environment | Clarity of communication, cross-functional effectiveness, influencing without authority |

**Note:** People Management is explicitly NOT assessed at conversion — that's evaluated on the job, not at this gate.

---

## The 5 Disciplines (backbone of Craft & Execution assessment)

- **Define** — Problem framing, strategic context, user needs, opportunity sizing, turning ambiguity into a clear problem worth solving
- **Discovery** — User research, insight synthesis, constraint mapping, landscape analysis, evidence generation
- **Validate** — Hypothesis design, MVP scoping, assumption testing, feedback loop design, de-risking before build
- **Build** — Delivery execution, story/ticket writing, backlog management, XFN alignment, shipping with eng/design
- **Measure** — Success metric definition, outcome tracking, post-launch learning, closing the loop back to Define

Without all five, teams either build confidently in the wrong direction, or ship and never learn if it landed.

## Conversion Process — 3 Phases

| Phase | Task | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Simplified 1-pager (product strategy) | Same as Phase 1, lighter version — strategic thinking, problem-solving clarity, product vision, risk assessment |
| **1** | Full 1-pager strategy document | Problem statement, vision of success, strategy, key risks — written form |
| **2** | 60-min interview: (1) Experience deep dive, (2) Live case study | Deep dive = product sense, execution, analytical thinking, communication. Live case = strategic thinking, clarity, communication under time pressure |

### Phase 0/1 — 1-Pager Guidance

- **The Problem** — what it is, why it matters, why now. Ground in real product data/examples.
- **Vision of Success** — how you know it's solved. Be specific, no vague language or untethered assertions.
- **Strategy and Approach** — how you'd solve it. Show, don't tell — use examples.
- **Risks and Mitigation** — what could go wrong. Connect decisions to user impact and org outcomes.

### Phase 2a — Experience Deep Dive Guidance

- Define the problem and ground it in evidence — prepare 2-3 detailed examples, know the numbers
- Structure answers: **Context → Challenge → Action → Result → Learnings**
- Be specific about *your* contribution, not just the team's
- Be honest about what didn't work and what you'd change
- Know your target audience, the evidence/data behind your problem statement, and the impact
- Have metrics ready that clearly tie back to the problem statement

### Phase 2b — Live Case Study Guidance

- Listen carefully, ask clarifying questions before diving in
- Define the problem before jumping to solutions
- Structure: **Problem → Success Definition → MVP → Risks**
- Be comfortable with ambiguity — rarely one right answer
- Adapt as new information comes in — rigidity is a signal
- Think aloud — show reasoning, not just conclusions

## Rubric Levels (L1–L6)

L1 (PM) → L2 (PM II) → L3 (Senior PM) → L4 (Lead PM) → L5 (Principal PM) → L6 (Director)

**Michelle's target: L2 (PM II) by EOY 2026** (per Jace check-in, 2026-07-30).

### What L2 (PM II) looks like across dimensions (condensed):

**1-Pager / Written Strategy:**
- *Problem Statement:* States the core problem but may still blur related issues; limited reasoning on why it matters now
- *Definition of Success:* Basic outcome measures (engagement, cost); may lean on lagging indicators without clarity on how they track problem-solving progress
- *Strategy:* Logical strategy that somewhat aligns with the problem; initial awareness of alternatives, lacks clear justification/data
- *Risk Identification:* Identifies relevant execution risks (low adoption, tech complexity); limited prioritization or mitigation planning
- *Communication:* Clear and logical; room for polish or depth

**Experience Deep Dive (5 Disciplines):**
- *Define:* States problem clearly, light awareness of user/business impact
- *Discovery:* Gathers user/stakeholder inputs, identifies patterns with support
- *Validate:* Proposes basic validation approaches
- *Build:* Writes clear user stories/tickets, participates in backlog grooming, identifies basic trade-offs
- *Measure:* Defines success linked to the problem being solved
- *Technical/Architectural:* Participates in technical discussions, high-level system understanding
- *Trade-Offs:* Identifies basic trade-offs, decision-making still evolving
- *Navigating Complexity:* Overcomes execution hurdles, adapts tactically
- *Cross-Functional Leadership:* Works effectively cross-functionally, builds reliable relationships with eng/design
- *Reflections:* Identifies learning moments, applies manager feedback

**Live Case Study:**
- *Problem Statement:* States core problem clearly, may lightly touch on user/business impact
- *Definition of Success:* Can define success linked back to the problem being solved
- *MVP:* Proposes straightforward MVP, understands need for testing/validation
- *Risk ID:* Spots technical/adoption risks, some awareness of stakeholder friction
- *Communication:* Communicates ideas clearly; logical but may lack polish or executive presence

### What L3 (Senior PM) looks like — the next bar up, useful as a stretch target:

- Defines a focused problem grounded in user/operational impact, begins prioritizing over other issues
- Uses a mix of leading/lagging indicators tied to the problem space
- Proposes a viable strategy with user data/research support, acknowledges known alternatives
- Recognizes key blockers tied to strategy (policy constraints, integration challenges), begins prioritizing mitigations
- Synthesizes insights across user types, surfaces constraints (Discovery)
- Tests feasibility and core assumptions, manages scope constraints (Validate)
- Writes well-structured tickets with acceptance criteria, leads backlog grooming, partners with engineering (Build)
- Identifies metrics across user archetypes (Measure)
- Coordinates across multiple teams, mentors juniors (Cross-Functional Leadership)
